Meet Leo · the human half

I'm not anyone special. I've just done this myself.

I've been a software engineer for about 15 years. I was the fourth engineer at Copy.ai, before ChatGPT. Then I got fired.

That's when I stopped thinking about AI as a way to do more work, and started asking how it could make work stop needing me.

The short version

Two questions people should ask me.

1. Do you actually know AI?

I worked on machine learning in healthcare data before the current wave. I joined Copy.ai as its fourth engineer and spent two and a half years there as generative AI went from a curiosity to a market. Since then I've built and run my own agent, Marvin, every day.

2. Can you turn that into a business?

That one I'm proving in public. On October 1, 2026 I reset my business to zero, set a fixed daily burn, and started publishing the whole funnel: what it costs, what it earns, and what the agents did. You can watch the number move.

How I think

Less work, not more software.

Most people use AI to produce more: more content, more email, more automations to babysit. I think that's backwards.

An agent is leverage only if you can stop checking its work. Evals are how you earn the right to stop checking.

I'm good at building complex systems and historically bad at explaining them simply. So I hold myself to the same rule I'd hold your agent to: show the artifact, show the score, show where it fails.
