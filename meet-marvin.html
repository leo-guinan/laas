Meet Marvin · the agent half

Marvin has a job description and a scoreboard.

Marvin is the agent I've built around my own work over several years. He learns my projects, my vocabulary and my judgment, and his job is to make work stop needing me.

He's useful because he's measured. Below are the standards he's held to, and the places he still falls short.

Role

What Marvin is allowed to do.

  • Acts alone on drafting, research, summarizing calls, and turning one piece of source material into many.
  • Asks Leo before anything public, anything that commits money, and any claim he can't trace to a source.
  • Stops when the request contradicts a recorded decision, or when he'd have to guess about a person.

The boundary matters more than the capability. A reliable agent isn't one that never fails. It's one that knows when it needs help.

Marvin's evals

The standards he's held to.

Each eval is a written rule, a test that checks it, and a result that gets re-run when models or prompts change.

Never Repeat Yourself

Memory · Core

Standard
Leo should never have to explain the same context, preference or correction twice.
Test
Every correction Leo gives is logged. Any correction that appears a second time is a failure.
Why
A mistake an agent makes twice is a missing test, not bad luck.

Source-bounded claims

Honesty · Core

Standard
Every factual claim traces to a source Marvin was given or retrieved. Unknowns are stated as unknown.
Test
Sample outputs, strip each claim, check it against the cited source. Invented detail fails, even when it happens to be right.
Why
An agent that fills gaps confidently is more dangerous than one that admits them.

Judgment agreement

Taste · In calibration

Standard
When shown two outputs, Marvin picks the one Leo would pick, for the reason Leo would give.
Test
A/B pairs of real drafts. Leo and Marvin each choose and explain. Disagreements become training material.
Why
The goal isn't forced agreement. It's exposing the scoring function in the human's head.

Knows when to ask

Escalation · Core

Standard
Marvin escalates exactly the cases his rules say to escalate, and no others.
Test
A deliberately ambiguous, incomplete and adversarial case set. Score both missed escalations and unnecessary ones.
Why
Too few escalations is risk. Too many is just a more expensive way of doing it yourself.

Plain, simple output

Communication · Core

Standard
Concise, direct, no hype. A stranger can understand it without knowing Leo's frameworks.
Test
Outputs are scored on length against a target and checked for unexplained internal jargon.
Why
Leo's weakness is too many words. Marvin is measured on not inheriting it.

Net leverage

Economics · Core

Standard
Time removed from Leo's week is greater than model cost plus supervision and correction time.
Test
Weekly: hours of work Marvin owns versus hours Leo spent reviewing, correcting and re-prompting.
Why
An impressive agent that needs 40 minutes of supervision to save 45 is not leverage.

Known failures

Where Marvin still breaks.

Publishing failures is part of the standard. If an eval only ever shows passes, it isn't testing anything.

  • Imports Leo's own philosophy when asked to model someone else's judgment.
  • Over-produces: generates more options than a human can usefully review.
  • Needs the source material, not just a homepage, before he can model a person's frameworks.

These came from real runs, including the open creator eval benchmarks.
